# ADR-0072 — Real FFmpeg in CI: the media suites

Status: Accepted (DS-2, 2026-10-20). Part of the AI-generated disclosure plan
the product owner approved on 2026-10-20
(`/mnt/project-files/cto/disclosure-proposal.md`).
Amends: ADR-0050 Decision 17's note that CI does not need `ffmpeg`. The
composer and the inspector stay dormant; only tests run them.

## Context

The deliverable composer (ADR-0050) and the media inspector are tested through
a fake process runner. Those suites pin every argument and every outcome, but
nothing has run the real `ffmpeg` or `ffprobe` against them, so nothing proves
that the arguments do what their comments say: that a clip of another shape is
letterboxed rather than cropped, that a short clip holds its last frame, that
the cut falls on the scene boundary, that audio is dropped, and that the
inspector reads the result correctly.

DS-5 will lay the disclosure image for the frame size over the video at the
origin (ADR-0071 Decision 6). Before the composer depends on that, the method
needs evidence that the committed images survive a real encode at composition
profile v1's settings: H.264 at CRF 18 in `yuv420p`, which halves the colour
resolution.

## Decision 1 — A separate suite, run where FFmpeg is installed

The suites live in `tests/media/*.media.test.ts` and run with
`pnpm test:media` (`vitest.media.config.ts`). `pnpm test` excludes them, as it
excludes the database suites, so a unit run needs no binaries. A run without
FFmpeg never passes: each suite's setup step cannot make its inputs, so Vitest
marks the file failed and reports its tests as skipped, and the run fails.

## Decision 2 — CI installs Ubuntu 24.04's FFmpeg in a job of its own

The `media` job runs on `ubuntu-24.04`, installs the distribution's `ffmpeg`
package (6.1.1) and prints its version before the suites run. The runner label
is pinned rather than `ubuntu-latest`, so FFmpeg's version moves only when
someone changes the label; Ubuntu's updates can still change the package's
revision, and the printed line records which one ran. `apt-get` retries a
failed download three times. The job uses no secret, no network media and no
provider.

## Decision 3 — Synthetic inputs only

Every input is generated by FFmpeg in the test (solid-colour clips, two of
which turn another colour partway, and a sine tone) or is a committed
disclosure image. No customer photo, provider output or
downloaded media is used. Each suite works in a temporary directory it removes.

## Decision 4 — What the composer suite proves

For each of the six composition profile v1 rasters, the real composer encodes
two clips. The first is 16:9 at 25 fps with a tone, two seconds long, its
timestamps starting at 3 seconds: blue, turning green at 1.6 seconds, and given
a 1.5-second scene. The second is 9:16 at 24 fps, one second long: red, turning
yellow for its last two frames, and given a two-second scene. Then:

- the real inspector measures exactly the frame size, 3,500 ms, one video
  stream and no audio;
- `ffprobe` reports H.264, `yuv420p`, 30/1 fps, square pixels and 105 frames;
- the 16:9 clip fills the width and is letterboxed in a taller frame, and the
  9:16 clip fills the height and is pillarboxed in a wider one: nothing is
  cropped;
- 1.8 seconds into the second scene, after its source ended, the frame is
  yellow: the clip holds its last frame, not its first, and does not loop;
- frame 44 is blue and frame 45 red: the cut is exactly at 1.5 seconds, and the
  first clip is trimmed from its start (its last 1.5 seconds would show green
  by frame 44).

## Decision 5 — What the overlay suite proves, and the method DS-5 adopts

Each of the six committed images is laid over a one-second clip of its size,
on nine backgrounds: mid grey, a near-white floor (`#f2f2f2`), a near-black
room (`#202020`), and the colours of a blue sky, a green garden, red brick, a
yellow wall, beige plaster and dark wood. With:

```text
[video][image]overlay=0:0:format=auto:shortest=1,format=yuv420p
```

the image looped as the second input, and encoded at profile v1's preset, CRF
and pixel format. FFmpeg decodes the image itself, and a decoded frame is
compared with the image's straight-alpha blend over the background as it
decodes (the frame's corner, well clear of the text).

The comparison is of lightness: BT.601 luma, the plane `yuv420p` keeps at full
resolution. The colour planes are halved in each direction, so a saturated
background's colour spreads into the text's edges. There, the largest error in
one colour channel is 36 to 68 levels on the blue, green, red and yellow
backgrounds (7 to 10 on average), 20 to 26 on beige and dark wood, and 8 to 15
on the neutrals. Any text over colour in this format does the same, and luma
leaves it out: decoding with the same matrix cancels the colour planes from it.
The text's legibility rests on its lightness, which stays close. The rest of
the frame is compared in every channel.

| Measure | Bound | Measured (FFmpeg 6.1.1) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| mean lightness error where the image has ink | 3 of 255 | 1.31 to 1.69 |
| largest lightness error there | 24 | 6.3 to 16.6 |
| largest change in any channel beyond 16 pixels of the ink | 2 | 0 |
| the strongest 1% of the text, against the background | at least 72 apart | 82 (mid grey) to 158 |
| the decoded background, against the colour asked for | 6 per channel | 0 to 3 |

The bounds are loose against the measurements, so a patch release of FFmpeg
or x264 does not trip them, and tight against what goes wrong: an image whose
transparency is ignored, laid at the wrong place, or scaled is tens of levels
out across the whole text.

The overlay graph is the method, written in the test. DS-5 moves it into the
composer, which lays the image over the Clean Master (ADR-0071 Decision 7),
and the suite then drives the composer instead.

## Decision 6 — Production FFmpeg is not decided here

Which FFmpeg the worker image carries is a deployment decision for when
composition is activated. The suites record the version they were measured
on, and that deployment runs them against its own FFmpeg before it composes
anything (`docs/decisions/TODO.md`).

## Consequences

- CI gains a third job, about a minute of installation and half a minute of
  encoding.
- A change to the composer's arguments now has to keep real video correct,
  not only match the pinned vector.
- The disclosure's survival through an encode is measured, not assumed, and
  the measurement is in the repository.
